Modelled runout (everywhere)

Snow is released from everything steeper than the release angle and allowed to run downhill while the line back to its start zone stays steeper than the alpha angle. 18° is the conservative field rule; 20–22° is closer to what most paths actually do. Computed in your browser from the same coarse elevation tiles, so it is an indication of where runout is plausible, not a hazard map.

Base map

Kartverket only covers Norway, so "follow the map" swaps to OpenTopoMap once you leave it.

The corner shows a scale bar and, when the base map draws named contours, its published interval for the zoom you are at: 20 m on Kartverket topo, 10 m on OpenTopoMap from zoom 15 and coarser below. Raster tiles carry no metadata, so this is the map series' equidistance, not something read out of the tile, and it can differ locally. The ruler button measures straight legs between the points you tap.

Slope is computed in your browser from open 10–30 m elevation tiles, so it is coarser than Kartverket's 1 m laser data. Treat the low classes as indicative and verify steep terrain against the official layer for the country you are in.

Before you use this

Slope colors are calculated from coarse open elevation data and can be several degrees off. The avalanche terrain layer is a model. The heatmap only shows where people happened to record activities. None of it knows anything about today's snowpack, weather, or what has already slid.

The locate button shows your GPS position, a circle for its uncertainty, and a dashed line along whichever way the phone is pointed. The compass is magnetic, drifts near metal and electronics, and points a couple of degrees off true north in Norway. It is a rough pointer, not a bearing you should navigate on.

Use it to find ideas worth investigating, then read the forecast at varsom.no, bring the gear and the training to use it, and make your own decisions in the terrain. This is not an avalanche safety tool, comes with no guarantee of any kind, and is used entirely at your own risk.
